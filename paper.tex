\begin{abstract}
For \(a>0\), we prove the \(d=4\) case of a conjecture of Felder and
Hoppe: every leading Gram matrix of their indefinite Hermitian form with
density \(\exp\bigl(-a|z|^2+\frac{\ii}{4}(z^4+\bar z^4)\bigr)\) is
nondegenerate, and all signed norms of the corresponding orthogonal
polynomials are positive.  Consequently, the solution of the quartic
discrete Painlev\'e-type recurrence arising from the quantum minimal
surface \(w_2=w_1^3\), with initial data given by Bessel functions of
orders \(\pm\frac14\), is positive, and we show that it is the only
positive solution of the recurrence.  The proof writes the Bessel
amplitude as a positive Laplace integral and uses independent source
parameters for \(z^4\) and \(\bar z^4\) to express each of the four
symmetry blocks through a bi-moment matrix with a positive weight.  A
triangular factorization and Andr\'eief's identity then give every
leading block minor as a prescribed sign times an explicit positive
multiple integral.  Uniqueness follows from a weighted energy estimate
for the convex variational structure of the recurrence.

\vskip5pt
\noindent\textbf{Keywords.}\enspace discrete Painlev\'e-type
recurrence; indefinite orthogonal polynomials; quartic potential;
bi-moment determinant; quantum minimal surface.

\vskip5pt
\noindent\textbf{MSC2020 Classification.}\enspace
34M55; 39A36; 33C45; 15B52; 53A10.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Discrete Painlev\'e-type recurrences occur both in matrix
regularizations of minimal surfaces and as recurrences for the
coefficients of orthogonal polynomials.  For the quantum minimal
surfaces associated with the holomorphic curves \(w_2=w_1^{d-1}\),
recurrences of this type were obtained in
\cite{CornalbaTaylor1998,ArnlindHoppeKontsevich2019,Hoppe2025} (see
\cite[Section~3.1]{FelderHoppe2025}).  The quartic recurrence studied
here is written explicitly in \cite[equation~(38)]{Hoppe2025} and
\cite[Section~5]{ClarksonEtAl2025}; those works also treat the positive
special-function solution in the cubic case \(d=3\).

Felder and Hoppe \cite{FelderHoppe2025} constructed an indefinite
Hermitian form for each \(d\) and showed that, whenever its leading Gram
matrices are nondegenerate, the ratios of its orthogonal-polynomial
norms obey
\begin{equation}
V_n+\frac1{a^2}\sum_{j=0}^{d-2}
\prod_{k=0}^{d-2}V_{n+j-k}=\frac{n+1}{a}.
\label{eq:FH-recurrence}
\end{equation}
For \(d=4\), after setting \(v_n=V_n/a\) and
\(\epsilon=a^{-2}\), the recurrence becomes
\begin{equation}
v_n\left(
1+v_{n-1}v_{n-2}
+v_{n+1}v_{n-1}
+v_{n+2}v_{n+1}
\right)
=(n+1)\epsilon ,
\qquad n\geq0,
\label{eq:quartic-recurrence}
\end{equation}
where \(v_n=0\) for \(n<0\).  The two initial values are
\begin{equation}
v_0=-\frac1a\frac{h'(a)}{h(a)},
\qquad
v_1=-\frac1a\frac{h''(a)}{h'(a)},
\label{eq:initial-values}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
h(a)=\frac{\pi^2a}{4}\bigg[
&J_{-1/4}\left(\frac{a^2}{4}\right)^2
-\sqrt2\,J_{-1/4}\left(\frac{a^2}{4}\right)
J_{1/4}\left(\frac{a^2}{4}\right)\\
&+J_{1/4}\left(\frac{a^2}{4}\right)^2
\bigg].
\end{split}
\label{eq:h-bessel}
\end{equation}
This is the function \(h(a)=\int_\C\rho_a\dd A\) of
\cite[equation~(3.4)]{FelderHoppe2025} for \(d=4\) (the density
\(\rho_a\) is recalled in \eqref{eq:density}), in the Bessel form given
in \cite[Section~4]{FelderHoppe2025}; Lemma~\ref{lem:bessel}
below rederives it.
Felder and Hoppe proved the required Gram nondegeneracy for
\(d=1,2,3\) and conjectured it for every \(d\)
\cite[Conjecture~3.6 and Theorem~3.7]{FelderHoppe2025}; the quartic
case is the first one not covered by their argument.  Their conjecture
is stated for \(a\geq0\).  We treat \(a>0\), where all planar moments
converge absolutely; at \(a=0\) they do not, and the endpoint would
require an oscillatory or regularized interpretation, which we do not
discuss.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Let \(a>0\).
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textup{(i)}] Every leading Gram matrix of the Felder--Hoppe
Hermitian form for \(d=4\) \textup{(Section~\ref{sec:gram})} is
nondegenerate, and all its signed norms \(\fh_n\) are positive.  Thus
\cite[Conjecture~3.6]{FelderHoppe2025} holds for \(d=4\) and \(a>0\).
\item[\textup{(ii)}] The initial values
\eqref{eq:initial-values}--\eqref{eq:h-bessel} are well defined and
extend uniquely to a real sequence satisfying
\eqref{eq:quartic-recurrence} for every \(n\geq0\).  This sequence is
\(v_n=\fh_{n+1}/(a\fh_n)\); in particular, \(v_n(a)>0\) for every
\(n\geq0\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
Uniqueness in (ii) refers to the prescribed initial data.  The next
theorem shows that the positive solution itself is unique, so that the
Bessel values \eqref{eq:initial-values} are the only initial values
leading to a positive solution.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:unique}
For every \(\epsilon>0\), there is at most one sequence of positive
numbers \((v_n)_{n\geq0}\) satisfying \eqref{eq:quartic-recurrence} for
every \(n\geq0\).  In particular, for \(a>0\) and \(\epsilon=a^{-2}\),
the sequence of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is the unique positive solution
of \eqref{eq:quartic-recurrence}.
\end{theorem}
For the cubic analogue, the existence of a positive solution was
proved in \cite[Section~7]{ArnlindHoppeKontsevich2019} and its uniqueness in
\cite[Theorem~2.1, proved in Section~4.2]{ClarksonEtAl2025}; for the quartic recurrence,
\cite[Section~5]{ClarksonEtAl2025} reports preliminary evidence for a
positive solution with analogous properties.  Our proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:unique} (Section~\ref{sec:unique}) uses only the
recurrence and is independent of the rest of the paper: in logarithmic
variables the recurrence is the stationarity condition of a convex
functional, and a weighted energy estimate controls the boundary at
infinity.

The proof is an integral sign mechanism that treats all indices at
once.  The square map \(z\mapsto z^2\) and Gaussian integrals turn the
quartic planar integral with coupling \(\lambda\) into a positive
Laplace integral (Lemma~\ref{lem:positive-source}).  Independent source
parameters \(u\) and \(v\) for \(z^4\) and \(\bar z^4\) are used on
disks, where the two-source integral is entire and, by rotation
invariance, depends only on \(uv\).  In exponential coordinates
\(u=\e^{t_1}\), \(v=\e^{t_2}\) this turns the Gram block of each residue
class modulo \(4\), after a unitriangular Stirling transformation, into
the Hankel matrix of derivatives of a positive kernel \(F_r(t)\)
(Lemmas~\ref{lem:four-kernels} and~\ref{lem:source-moments}).
Invertible triangular row and column transformations reduce these
Hankel matrices to bi-moment matrices
\[
\left[
\int_0^\infty s^p
\left(\frac{s^2}{1+s^2}\right)^q\dd\mu_r(s)
\right]_{p,q=0}^{k-1}
\]
with positive measures \(\mu_r\).  Since \(s\) and \(s^2/(1+s^2)\) are
both strictly increasing, Andr\'eief's identity writes their
determinants as positive integrals (Lemma~\ref{lem:leading-source-signs}).
This fixes the sign of every leading block minor and gives an explicit
positive multiple-integral formula for it
(Proposition~\ref{prop:block-determinants}).  The determinant step is a
continuous Cauchy--Binet argument of the kind used for biorthogonal
ensembles \cite{Borodin1998}.  To our knowledge, the new ingredient is the
two-source representation: differentiation in a common quartic coupling
mixes the insertions of \(z^4\) and \(\bar z^4\), whereas the \((p,q)\)
entry of a block requires their powers separately, and independent
source derivatives select them.  The
construction uses two features of \(d=4\): the square map makes the
quartic phase quadratic, and one integration by parts makes the kernels
of the residue classes \(2\) and \(3\) manifestly positive.  We do not
consider higher \(d\) here.

Section~\ref{sec:gram} recalls the quartic Hermitian form.
Section~\ref{sec:sign-lemma} proves the Hankel determinant identity.
Section~\ref{sec:blocks} derives the four positive kernels and evaluates
the block determinants.  Section~\ref{sec:bessel} identifies the planar
integral with the Bessel combination \eqref{eq:h-bessel}.
Section~\ref{sec:completion} identifies the norm ratios with the
solution of \eqref{eq:quartic-recurrence}, and Section~\ref{sec:unique}
proves Theorem~\ref{thm:unique}.  Theorems~\ref{thm:main}
and~\ref{thm:unique} have been formally verified in Lean~4.33.0 with
Mathlib, as the declarations \path{Painleve.main_theorem} and
\path{Painleve.unique_theorem}; the formalization follows the arguments
given here, and the code availability statement at the end identifies
the release.

\section{The quartic Gram form}\label{sec:gram}

Fix \(a>0\) and put
\begin{equation}
\rho_a(z)=
\exp\left(-a|z|^2+\frac{\ii}{4}(z^4+\bar z^4)\right),
\label{eq:density}
\end{equation}
the density of \cite[Section~3.1]{FelderHoppe2025} for \(d=4\).  The
Gaussian factor makes all polynomial moments absolutely convergent.  Let
\(\eta=\e^{\pi\ii/4}\).  Since \(\eta^4=-1\),
\[
\rho_a(\ii z)=\rho_a(z),
\qquad
\rho_a(\eta z)=\overline{\rho_a(z)}.
\]
Decompose the polynomial ring into \(C_4\)-weight spaces
\[
\C[z]=\bigoplus_{r=0}^3\mathcal P_r,
\qquad
\mathcal P_r=\{f\in\C[z]:f(\ii z)=\ii^r f(z)\}
=\Span\{z^{r+4q}:q\geq0\}.
\]
For \(f\in\mathcal P_r\), set \((\sigma f)(z)=(-\eta)^{-r}f(\eta z)\),
extend \(\sigma\) linearly to \(\C[z]\), and define
\begin{equation}
(f,g)_a
=\int_{\C}f(z)\overline{\sigma g(z)}\rho_a(z)\dd A(z),
\qquad
\dd A=\dd(\operatorname{Re}z)\dd(\operatorname{Im}z),
\label{eq:pairing}
\end{equation}
which is linear in \(f\) and conjugate-linear in \(g\).  This is the
Hermitian form of \cite[Proposition~3.1]{FelderHoppe2025} for \(d=4\),
with the right inverse \(\chi(r)=r\) \((0\leq r\leq3)\) chosen there.  If
\(m=r+4q\) with \(0\leq r\leq3\), then
\begin{equation}
\sigma z^m=(-\eta)^{-r}\eta^m z^m=(-1)^{r+q}z^m.
\label{eq:twist-sign}
\end{equation}
Since \(\sigma\) preserves each \(\mathcal P_r\) and
\(\rho_a(\ii z)=\rho_a(z)\), the substitution \(z\mapsto\ii z\) shows
that distinct weight spaces are orthogonal.  On \(\mathcal P_r\), in the
basis \(z^r,z^{r+4},\ldots\), the Gram matrix is therefore
\begin{equation}
G_{pq}^{(r)}
=(z^{r+4p},z^{r+4q})_a=(-1)^{r+q}M_{pq}^{(r)},
\qquad
M_{pq}^{(r)}=\int_{\C}
z^{r+4p}\bar z^{r+4q}\rho_a(z)\dd A(z).
\label{eq:gram-block}
\end{equation}
The substitution \(z\mapsto\eta z\) and
\(\rho_a(\eta z)=\overline{\rho_a(z)}\) give
\(\overline{M_{qp}^{(r)}}=(-1)^{q-p}M_{pq}^{(r)}\), so each \(G^{(r)}\) is
a Hermitian matrix; with the orthogonality of the weight spaces, this
confirms directly that \((\ ,\ )_a\) is Hermitian.

For \(k\geq1\), let
\begin{equation}
\Delta_k^{(r)}
=\det[G_{pq}^{(r)}]_{p,q=0}^{k-1},
\qquad
\Delta_0^{(r)}=1.
\label{eq:Delta}
\end{equation}
Assume for the moment that all \(\Delta_k^{(r)}\) are nonzero.  After
the monomials \(1,z,\ldots,z^N\) are grouped by residue modulo \(4\),
their Gram matrix is block diagonal, with leading principal submatrices
of the \(G^{(r)}\) as blocks.  Hence every leading Gram matrix
\(\big[(z^p,z^q)_a\big]_{p,q=0}^{N}\) is nondegenerate, and Gram--Schmidt
gives unique monic orthogonal polynomials \(P_n(z)=z^n+\cdots\).
Orthogonalizing \(z^n\) against lower monomials subtracts only
components in the weight space of \(z^n\), so
\(P_n\in\mathcal P_{n\bmod4}\).  Following
\cite[Lemma~3.4 and Example~3.5]{FelderHoppe2025}, for \(\ell\in\mathbb Z\)
put
\begin{equation}
\tau(\ell)=
\begin{cases}
-1,&\ell\equiv3\pmod4,\\
1,&\text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\qquad
\varrho(n)=(-1)^n\prod_{\ell=0}^{n-1}\tau(\ell)
=(-1)^{n+\lfloor n/4\rfloor}.
\label{eq:rho-sign}
\end{equation}
The signed norms \(\fh_n\) of \cite[Section~3.1]{FelderHoppe2025},
denoted \(h_n\) there, are defined by
\begin{equation}
(P_n,P_n)_a=\varrho(n)\fh_n.
\label{eq:signed-norm}
\end{equation}
The matrices \(\big[(z^p,z^q)_a\big]_{p,q=0}^{N}\) are exactly the Gram
matrices of \cite[Conjecture~3.6]{FelderHoppe2025}.
Section~\ref{sec:blocks} proves that all \(\Delta_k^{(r)}\) are nonzero
and that \(\fh_n>0\).

\section{A Hankel determinant identity}
\label{sec:sign-lemma}

Let \(y>0\), \(\alpha\geq0\), \(\beta\geq0\), and \(b=\beta+\frac12\).
For \(w>0\), define
\begin{equation}
\Phi_{\alpha,\beta}(w;y)
=\int_0^\infty
\e^{-ys}s^{\alpha-1/2}(1+ws^2)^{-b}\dd s .
\label{eq:Phi}
\end{equation}
Let \(\mu\) be the measure
\(\dd\mu(s)=\e^{-ys}s^{\alpha-1/2}(1+s^2)^{-b}\dd s\) on \((0,\infty)\),
put \(\psi(s)=s^2/(1+s^2)\), and define the bi-moments
\begin{equation}
B_{pq}(y)
=\int_0^\infty
s^p\psi(s)^q\dd\mu(s).
\label{eq:B-moments}
\end{equation}
These integrals converge absolutely because \(\alpha\geq0\) and \(y>0\),
and they may be differentiated under the integral sign, locally
uniformly in \(w,y>0\): the derivative
\(\partial_w^j(1+ws^2)^{-b}=(-1)^j(b)_js^{2j}(1+ws^2)^{-b-j}\) has
absolute value at most \((b)_jw^{-j}\), and each \(y\)-derivative
multiplies the integrand by \(-s\).  Here
\((b)_j=b(b+1)\cdots(b+j-1)\) is the rising Pochhammer symbol.  In
particular, \(\Phi_{\alpha,\beta}(\cdot\,;y)\) is smooth on \((0,\infty)\),
and \(\partial_y^iB_{0q}=(-1)^iB_{iq}\).  We write \(S_k=k(k-1)/2\) and
\(\stir{q}{j}\) for the Stirling numbers of the second kind, defined by
\(\stir00=1\), \(\stir q j=0\) for \(j>q\) or \(j=0<q\), and
\(\stir{q+1}{j}=j\stir qj+\stir q{j-1}\).

\begin{lemma}[Hankel determinants of the kernel]
\label{lem:leading-source-signs}
Let \(F(t)=\Phi_{\alpha,\beta}(\e^t;y)\).  For every \(k\geq1\),
\begin{equation}
(-1)^{S_k}\det\left[F^{(p+q)}(0)\right]_{p,q=0}^{k-1}
=\left(\frac y2\right)^{S_k}
\prod_{q=0}^{k-1}(b)_q\,
\det[B_{pq}]_{p,q=0}^{k-1},
\label{eq:triangular-factorization}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
\det[B_{pq}]_{p,q=0}^{k-1}
=\frac1{k!}\int_{(0,\infty)^k}
\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq k}(s_j-s_i)\bigl(\psi(s_j)-\psi(s_i)\bigr)
\prod_{j=1}^k\dd\mu(s_j)>0.
\label{eq:andreief}
\end{equation}
In particular,
\(\sgn\det[F^{(p+q)}(0)]_{p,q=0}^{k-1}=(-1)^{S_k}\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For a function \(G\) that is smooth on \((0,\infty)\), induction on \(q\)
with the recurrence for \(\stir qj\) gives
\begin{equation}
\frac{\dd^q}{\dd t^q}G(\e^t)
=\sum_{j=0}^q\stir qj\e^{jt}G^{(j)}(\e^t),
\label{eq:stirling}
\end{equation}
because \(\frac{\dd}{\dd t}\bigl[\e^{jt}G^{(j)}(\e^t)\bigr]
=j\e^{jt}G^{(j)}(\e^t)+\e^{(j+1)t}G^{(j+1)}(\e^t)\).  Differentiating
\eqref{eq:Phi} \(j\) times in \(w\) and substituting \(s=x/\sqrt w\), which
turns \((ws^2)^j(1+ws^2)^{-j}\) into \(\psi(x)^j\), gives
\begin{equation}
w^j\,\partial_w^j\Phi_{\alpha,\beta}(w;y)
=(-1)^j(b)_j\,w^{-\gamma}B_{0j}\bigl(yw^{-1/2}\bigr),
\qquad
\gamma=\frac\alpha2+\frac14.
\label{eq:Phi-scaling}
\end{equation}
Hence, by \eqref{eq:stirling} with \(G=\Phi_{\alpha,\beta}(\cdot\,;y)\),
\[
F^{(q)}(t)=\sum_{j=0}^q\stir qj(-1)^j(b)_j\,
\e^{-\gamma t}B_{0j}\bigl(y\e^{-t/2}\bigr).
\]
Next, for a function \(g\) that is smooth on \((0,\infty)\), put
\(Y=y\e^{-t/2}\); since
\(\frac{\dd}{\dd t}\bigl[\e^{-\gamma t}Y^ig^{(i)}(Y)\bigr]
=\e^{-\gamma t}\bigl[(-\gamma-\tfrac i2)Y^ig^{(i)}(Y)
-\tfrac12Y^{i+1}g^{(i+1)}(Y)\bigr]\), induction on \(p\) gives
\[
\left.\frac{\dd^p}{\dd t^p}\Bigl[\e^{-\gamma t}g\bigl(y\e^{-t/2}\bigr)\Bigr]
\right|_{t=0}
=\sum_{i=0}^p\kappa_{pi}\,y^ig^{(i)}(y)
\]
with constants \(\kappa_{pi}\) depending only on \(\gamma\), determined by
\(\kappa_{00}=1\) and
\(\kappa_{p+1,i}=(-\gamma-\frac i2)\kappa_{pi}-\frac12\kappa_{p,i-1}\),
with \(\kappa_{pi}=0\) for \(i<0\) or \(i>p\); in particular
\(\kappa_{pp}=(-\frac12)^p\).  Applying this to
\(g=B_{0j}\), with \(g^{(i)}=(-1)^iB_{ij}\), we obtain
\[
F^{(p+q)}(0)
=\sum_{i=0}^p\sum_{j=0}^q
\kappa_{pi}(-y)^i\,B_{ij}\,(-1)^j(b)_j\stir qj .
\]
Thus the \(k\times k\) truncations satisfy
\([F^{(p+q)}(0)]=TB\Lambda\Sigma^{\mathsf T}\), where
\(T_{pi}=\kappa_{pi}(-y)^i\) is lower triangular with
\(T_{pp}=(y/2)^p\),
\(\Lambda=\operatorname{diag}\bigl((-1)^j(b)_j\bigr)_{j=0}^{k-1}\), and
\(\Sigma_{qj}=\stir qj\) is lower triangular with ones on the diagonal.
Hence \(\det T=(y/2)^{S_k}\), \(\det\Sigma=1\), and
\(\det\Lambda=(-1)^{S_k}\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}(b)_j\).  This proves
\eqref{eq:triangular-factorization}.

Andr\'eief's identity \cite{Andreief1886,Forrester2019}, applied to the
functions \(s^p\) and \(\psi(s)^q\), all of whose products are
\(\mu\)-integrable, gives
\[
\det[B_{pq}]_{p,q=0}^{k-1}
=\frac1{k!}\int_{(0,\infty)^k}
\det[s_j^p]_{p=0,\ldots,k-1}^{j=1,\ldots,k}\,
\det[\psi(s_j)^q]_{q=0,\ldots,k-1}^{j=1,\ldots,k}
\prod_{j=1}^k\dd\mu(s_j).
\]
Both determinants are Vandermonde determinants, which gives the
integrand in \eqref{eq:andreief}.  Since \(s\) and \(\psi\) are strictly
increasing on \((0,\infty)\), this integrand is positive off the
diagonals \(s_i=s_j\), which are \(\mu^{\otimes k}\)-null, and \(\mu\)
has a positive density.  Hence \(\det B>0\).  Finally, \((b)_q>0\)
because \(b>0\).
\end{proof}

\section{The four positive kernels}\label{sec:blocks}

Throughout this section,
\[
y=\frac{a^2}{2},
\qquad
C=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}.
\]

\begin{lemma}[positive source]\label{lem:positive-source}
For every \(\lambda>0\),
\begin{equation}
\int_{\C}\exp\left(-a|z|^2+\frac{\ii\lambda}{4}(z^4+\bar z^4)\right)
\dd A(z)
=C\,\Phi_{0,0}(\lambda^2;y).
\label{eq:positive-source}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The integrand is bounded by \(\e^{-a|z|^2}\).  The map \(z\mapsto w=z^2\)
is two-to-one from \(\C\setminus\{0\}\) onto itself with Jacobian
\(4|z|^2=4|w|\), and \(z^4+\bar z^4=2(X^2-Y^2)\) for \(w=X+\ii Y\).
Hence the left-hand side of \eqref{eq:positive-source} equals
\[
\frac12\int_{\mathbb R^2}
\frac{\e^{-a|w|}}{|w|}\,
\e^{\ii\lambda(X^2-Y^2)/2}\dd X\dd Y.
\]
By \cite[equations~(10.32.10) and (10.39.2)]{NISTHandbook2010} with
\(\nu=-\frac12\),
\[
\frac{\e^{-a|w|}}{|w|}
=\frac1{\sqrt\pi}\int_0^\infty
\sigma^{-1/2}\exp\left(-\sigma|w|^2-\frac{a^2}{4\sigma}\right)\dd\sigma.
\]
The resulting triple integral converges absolutely, because
\(\int_{\mathbb R^2}\e^{-\sigma|w|^2}\dd X\dd Y=\pi/\sigma\) and
\(\int_0^\infty\sigma^{-3/2}\e^{-a^2/(4\sigma)}\dd\sigma<\infty\).  By
Fubini's theorem and the Gaussian integrals
\(\int_{\mathbb R}\e^{-(\sigma\mp\ii\lambda/2)X^2}\dd X
=\sqrt\pi\,(\sigma\mp\ii\lambda/2)^{-1/2}\) (principal branch), whose
product is \(\pi(\sigma^2+\lambda^2/4)^{-1/2}\), the left-hand side
equals
\[
\frac{\sqrt\pi}{2}\int_0^\infty
\sigma^{-1/2}\e^{-a^2/(4\sigma)}
\left(\sigma^2+\frac{\lambda^2}{4}\right)^{-1/2}\dd\sigma .
\]
The substitution \(\sigma=1/(2s)\), for which
\(\sigma^2+\lambda^2/4=(1+\lambda^2s^2)/(4s^2)\), turns this into
\[
C\int_0^\infty\frac{\e^{-ys}}{\sqrt{s(1+\lambda^2s^2)}}\dd s
=C\,\Phi_{0,0}(\lambda^2;y).
\qedhere
\]
\end{proof}

For \(r\in\{0,1,2,3\}\) and \(\lambda>0\), define the weighted sources
\[
\mathcal W_r(a;\lambda)
=\int_{\C}|z|^{2r}
\exp\left(-a|z|^2+\frac{\ii\lambda}{4}(z^4+\bar z^4)\right)
\dd A(z).
\]
Differentiation under the integral sign, dominated on \(a\geq a_0>0\) by
\(|z|^{2r+2}\e^{-a_0|z|^2}\), gives
\(\mathcal W_{r+1}=-\partial_a\mathcal W_r\) for \(r=0,1,2\), so
\(\mathcal W_r=(-\partial_a)^r\mathcal W_0\).

\begin{lemma}[four positive kernels]\label{lem:four-kernels}
Define
\begin{equation}
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
r&A_r&\alpha_r&\beta_r\\ \hline
0&C&0&0\\
1&aC&1&0\\
2&2C&1&1\\
3&2aC&2&1
\end{array}
\label{eq:block-parameters}
\end{equation}
Then \(\mathcal W_r(a;\lambda)=A_r\Phi_{\alpha_r,\beta_r}(\lambda^2;y)\)
for \(r=0,1,2,3\) and \(\lambda>0\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write \(w=\lambda^2\).  The case \(r=0\) is
Lemma~\ref{lem:positive-source}.  Since \(\partial_a=a\partial_y\) and
\(\partial_y\Phi_{\alpha,\beta}=-\Phi_{\alpha+1,\beta}\), the case
\(r=1\) follows.  Next, \((-\partial_a)^2=\partial_y+2y\partial_y^2\), so
\(\mathcal W_2=C\bigl(2y\Phi_{2,0}-\Phi_{1,0}\bigr)(w;y)\).  Integration
by parts, whose boundary terms vanish at \(0\) and \(\infty\), gives
\begin{equation}
y\int_0^\infty
\frac{\e^{-ys}s^{3/2}}{\sqrt{1+ws^2}}\dd s
=\frac12\int_0^\infty
\frac{\e^{-ys}s^{1/2}}{\sqrt{1+ws^2}}\dd s
+\int_0^\infty
\frac{\e^{-ys}s^{1/2}}{(1+ws^2)^{3/2}}\dd s,
\label{eq:ibp-kernel}
\end{equation}
that is, \(y\Phi_{2,0}=\frac12\Phi_{1,0}+\Phi_{1,1}\).  Hence
\(\mathcal W_2=2C\Phi_{1,1}\), and
\(\mathcal W_3=-a\partial_y\mathcal W_2=2aC\Phi_{2,1}\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[the Gram blocks in exponential coordinates]
\label{lem:source-moments}
For \(r\in\{0,1,2,3\}\) let
\(M_{kl}^{(r)}=\int_\C z^{r+4k}\bar z^{r+4l}\rho_a(z)\dd A(z)\) and
\(F_r(t)=A_r\Phi_{\alpha_r,\beta_r}(\e^t;y)\).  Then, for all
\(p,q\geq0\),
\begin{equation}
\sum_{k=0}^p\sum_{l=0}^q\stir pk\stir ql
\left(\frac{\ii}{4}\right)^{k+l}M_{kl}^{(r)}
=F_r^{(p+q)}(0).
\label{eq:source-moments}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For \(R>0\) and \(u,v\in\C\), define the disk integral
\[
\mathcal Z_R(u,v)
=\int_{|z|\leq R}|z|^{2r}
\exp\left(-a|z|^2+\frac{\ii}{4}(uz^4+v\bar z^4)\right)\dd A(z).
\]
Over the compact disk, all derivatives in \((u,v)\) may be taken under
the integral sign, and \(\mathcal Z_R\) is entire in each variable.

\emph{Step 1: \(\mathcal Z_R(u,v)=\mathcal Z_R(uv,1)\) for \(u,v>0\).}
For fixed \(u,v>0\), the function
\(H(s)=\mathcal Z_R(u\e^s,v\e^{-s})\) is entire in \(s\in\C\).  The
rotation \(z\mapsto\e^{\ii\varphi/4}z\), \(\varphi\in\mathbb R\),
preserves the disk, \(|z|\) and the area measure, and multiplies \(z^4\)
by \(\e^{\ii\varphi}\) and \(\bar z^4\) by \(\e^{-\ii\varphi}\); hence
\(H(s+\ii\varphi)=H(s)\).  Differentiating in \(\varphi\) at
\(\varphi=0\) gives \(\ii H'(s)=0\), so \(H\) is constant, and
\(H(0)=H(\log v)\) is the claim.

\emph{Step 2: the truncated identity.}  For real \(t_1,t_2\) put
\(K(t_1,t_2)=\mathcal Z_R(\e^{t_1},\e^{t_2})\).  By Step~1,
\(K(t_1,t_2)=f_R(t_1+t_2)\) with \(f_R(t)=\mathcal Z_R(\e^{t/2},\e^{t/2})\),
so \(\partial_{t_2}^q\partial_{t_1}^pK(0,0)=f_R^{(p+q)}(0)\).  On the other
hand, \eqref{eq:stirling} with \(G(w)=\exp(cw)\) gives
\(\frac{\dd^p}{\dd t^p}\exp(c\e^t)=\sum_k\stir pk(c\e^t)^k\exp(c\e^t)\);
applying this under the integral, first with \(c=\ii z^4/4\) and then
with \(c=\ii\bar z^4/4\), yields
\[
\partial_{t_2}^q\partial_{t_1}^pK(0,0)
=\sum_{k=0}^p\sum_{l=0}^q\stir pk\stir ql
\left(\frac{\ii}{4}\right)^{k+l}
\int_{|z|\leq R}z^{r+4k}\bar z^{r+4l}\rho_a(z)\dd A(z),
\]
where \(|z|^{2r}z^{4k}\bar z^{4l}=z^{r+4k}\bar z^{r+4l}\).

\emph{Step 3: \(R\to\infty\).}  The truncated moments converge to
\(M_{kl}^{(r)}\), since their integrands are bounded by
\(|z|^{2r+4k+4l}\e^{-a|z|^2}\).  For real \(t\), the factor
\(\exp\bigl(\frac{\ii}{4}\e^{t/2}(z^4+\bar z^4)\bigr)\) has modulus \(1\),
and by \eqref{eq:stirling} its \(t\)-derivatives of order \(m\) are this
factor times a polynomial in \(\e^{t/2}(z^4+\bar z^4)\); for \(|t|\leq1\)
they are bounded by \(c_m(1+|z|)^{4m}\).  Hence derivatives of all
orders may be taken under the integral both for \(f_R\) and for its
full-plane counterpart \(t\mapsto\mathcal W_r(a;\e^{t/2})\), and dominated
convergence gives
\(f_R^{(m)}(0)\to\frac{\dd^m}{\dd t^m}\mathcal W_r(a;\e^{t/2})|_{t=0}\).
By Lemma~\ref{lem:four-kernels} with \(\lambda=\e^{t/2}\),
\(\mathcal W_r(a;\e^{t/2})=F_r(t)\).  Letting \(R\to\infty\) in Step~2
proves \eqref{eq:source-moments}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[block determinants]
\label{prop:block-determinants}
For \(r\in\{0,1,2,3\}\) and \(k\geq1\), with the parameters
\eqref{eq:block-parameters},
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
(-1)^{rk+S_k}\Delta_k^{(r)}
={}&\frac{A_r^k(4a^2)^{S_k}}{k!}
\prod_{q=0}^{k-1}\left(\beta_r+\frac12\right)_q\\
&{}\times
\int_{(0,\infty)^k}
\prod_{j=1}^k
\frac{\e^{-ys_j}s_j^{\alpha_r-1/2}}
{(1+s_j^2)^{\beta_r+k-1/2}}
\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq k}
(s_j-s_i)^2(s_i+s_j)
\prod_{j=1}^k\dd s_j .
\end{split}
\label{eq:block-multiple-integral}
\end{equation}
The right-hand side is strictly positive, so
\(\sgn\Delta_k^{(r)}=(-1)^{rk+S_k}\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By \eqref{eq:gram-block},
\(G_{pq}^{(r)}=(-1)^{r+q}(4/\ii)^{p+q}\bigl[(\ii/4)^{p+q}M_{pq}^{(r)}\bigr]\).
Taking \((4/\ii)^p\) out of row \(p\) and \((-1)^{r+q}(4/\ii)^q\) out of
column \(q\), and using \((4/\ii)^2=-16\), gives
\[
\Delta_k^{(r)}
=(-1)^{rk}16^{S_k}
\det\left[\left(\frac\ii4\right)^{p+q}M_{pq}^{(r)}\right]_{p,q=0}^{k-1}.
\]
The left-hand side of \eqref{eq:source-moments} is the \((p,q)\) entry
of \(\Sigma\,[(\ii/4)^{k+l}M_{kl}^{(r)}]\,\Sigma^{\mathsf T}\), with
\(\Sigma\) the unitriangular Stirling matrix of
Lemma~\ref{lem:leading-source-signs}; its \(k\times k\) truncations
therefore have the same determinant.  By
Lemma~\ref{lem:source-moments} that determinant equals
\(\det[F_r^{(p+q)}(0)]=A_r^k\det[F^{(p+q)}(0)]\), where \(F\) is the
function of Lemma~\ref{lem:leading-source-signs} for
\((\alpha,\beta)=(\alpha_r,\beta_r)\).  Hence that lemma and
\(16^{S_k}(y/2)^{S_k}=(4a^2)^{S_k}\) give
\[
(-1)^{rk+S_k}\Delta_k^{(r)}
=A_r^k(4a^2)^{S_k}
\prod_{q=0}^{k-1}\left(\beta_r+\frac12\right)_q
\det[B_{pq}]_{p,q=0}^{k-1}.
\]
Finally,
\[
\psi(s_j)-\psi(s_i)
=\frac{(s_j-s_i)(s_j+s_i)}
{(1+s_i^2)(1+s_j^2)},
\]
and each factor \(1+s_j^2\) occurs \(k-1\) times in the product over
\(i<j\).  Substituting this into \eqref{eq:andreief} gives
\eqref{eq:block-multiple-integral}, and its positivity is part of
Lemma~\ref{lem:leading-source-signs}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[quartic Gram nondegeneracy]
\label{cor:positive-norms}
For every \(N\geq0\), the leading Gram matrix
\(\big[(z^p,z^q)_a\big]_{p,q=0}^{N}\) is nondegenerate, and the signed
norms in \eqref{eq:signed-norm} satisfy
\[
\fh_n>0\qquad(n\geq0).
\]
In particular, \cite[Conjecture~3.6]{FelderHoppe2025} holds for \(d=4\)
and \(a>0\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Proposition~\ref{prop:block-determinants}, every \(\Delta_k^{(r)}\)
is nonzero, so by Section~\ref{sec:gram} every leading Gram matrix is
nondegenerate and the polynomials \(P_n\) exist.  Let \(n=r+4j\).
Replacing \(z^n\) by \(P_n\) in the basis
\(z^r,z^{r+4},\ldots,z^{r+4j}\) is a unitriangular change of basis, and
orthogonality makes the new Gram matrix
\(\operatorname{diag}\bigl([G_{pq}^{(r)}]_{p,q=0}^{j-1},(P_n,P_n)_a\bigr)\).
Comparing determinants gives
\begin{equation}
(P_n,P_n)_a
=\frac{\Delta_{j+1}^{(r)}}{\Delta_j^{(r)}};
\label{eq:norm-delta-ratio}
\end{equation}
this Schur-complement identity uses only the nonvanishing of the
leading minors, not positive definiteness.  By
Proposition~\ref{prop:block-determinants},
\(\sgn(P_n,P_n)_a=(-1)^{r+j}\), and \eqref{eq:rho-sign} gives
\(\varrho(r+4j)=(-1)^{r+j}\).  Hence
\(\fh_n=\varrho(n)(P_n,P_n)_a>0\).  The last assertion is the
identification at the end of Section~\ref{sec:gram}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
By \eqref{eq:norm-delta-ratio}, \(\fh_n=|\Delta_{j+1}^{(r)}|/|\Delta_j^{(r)}|\)
for \(n=r+4j\), and \eqref{eq:block-multiple-integral} writes each
determinant as an integral of a positive function.  The norms, and the
ratios \(v_n=\fh_{n+1}/(a\fh_n)\) of Section~\ref{sec:completion}, can
therefore be evaluated without the cancellation inherent in the
oscillatory moment determinants.
\end{remark}

\section{The Bessel form of the initial function}\label{sec:bessel}

\begin{lemma}[Bessel form]\label{lem:bessel}
For every \(a>0\),
\(\int_\C\rho_a\dd A=h(a)\), the Bessel combination \eqref{eq:h-bessel}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Put \(H(a)=\int_\C\rho_a\dd A\) and
\(m_{pq}=\int_\C z^p\bar z^q\rho_a(z)\dd A(z)\) for \(a>0\).

\emph{Step 1: \(H'''+a^2H'+aH=0\) on \((0,\infty)\).}  Differentiation
under the integral sign gives \(H^{(k)}(a)=(-1)^km_{kk}\).  With the
Wirtinger derivative
\(\partial_z=\frac12(\partial_{\operatorname{Re}z}-\ii\partial_{\operatorname{Im}z})\),
\(\partial_z\bar z^q=0\) and
\(\partial_z\rho_a=(-a\bar z+\ii z^3)\rho_a\), so integrating
\(\partial_z(z^p\bar z^q\rho_a)\) over \(\C\), with no boundary terms
because of the Gaussian decay, gives
\[
p\,m_{p-1,q}+\ii\,m_{p+3,q}=a\,m_{p,q+1}.
\]
For \((p,q)=(1,0)\) and \((0,3)\) this reads
\(m_{00}+\ii m_{40}=am_{11}\) and \(\ii m_{33}=am_{04}\).  The
substitution \(z\mapsto\eta z\) of Section~\ref{sec:gram} gives
\(\overline{m_{qp}}=\bar\eta^{\,p}\eta^q m_{pq}\); hence every
\(m_{kk}\) is real and \(\overline{m_{40}}=-m_{04}\).  Therefore
\(m_{33}=am_{00}-a^2m_{11}\), which is the claimed equation.

\emph{Step 2: the Bessel side.}  For \(x>0\),
\(J_\nu(x)=(x/2)^\nu s_\nu(x)\) with the even entire function
\(s_\nu(x)=\sum_{k\geq0}(-1)^kx^{2k}/\bigl(4^kk!\,\Gamma(k+\nu+1)\bigr)\)
\cite[equation~(10.2.2)]{NISTHandbook2010}, and Bessel's equation
\cite[equation~(10.2.1)]{NISTHandbook2010} becomes
\(xs_\nu''+(2\nu+1)s_\nu'+xs_\nu=0\).  Writing \(s_\pm=s_{\pm1/4}\) and
\(x=a^2/4\), so that \((x/2)^{\mp1/4}=(a^2/8)^{\mp1/4}\), we get, for
\(a>0\),
\[
h(a)=h_S(a):=\frac{\pi^2}{4}\left[\sqrt8\,s_-(x)^2
-\sqrt2\,a\,s_-(x)s_+(x)+\frac{a^2}{\sqrt8}\,s_+(x)^2\right].
\]
The function \(h_S\) is smooth on \(\mathbb R\).  Put
\(U(a)=s_-(a^2/4)\) and \(V(a)=a\,s_+(a^2/4)\).  The equations for
\(s_\mp\), with \(2\nu+1=\frac12\) and \(\frac32\), give
\(U''+\frac{a^2}{4}U=0\) and \(V''+\frac{a^2}{4}V=0\).  If
\(y_1,y_2\) solve \(y''+qy=0\), then \(P=y_1y_2\) satisfies
\(P'''+4qP'+2q'P=0\); with \(q=a^2/4\) this is
\(P'''+a^2P'+aP=0\).  Since
\(h_S=\frac{\pi^2}{4}\bigl(\sqrt8\,U^2-\sqrt2\,UV+V^2/\sqrt8\bigr)\),
we get \(h_S'''+a^2h_S'+ah_S=0\) on \(\mathbb R\).  Since
\(s_\nu(0)=1/\Gamma(\nu+1)\) and \(s_\nu'(0)=0\),
\[
h_S(0)=\frac{\pi^2\sqrt8}{4\,\Gamma(\frac34)^2},
\qquad
h_S'(0)=-\frac{\pi^2\sqrt2}{4\,\Gamma(\frac34)\Gamma(\frac54)},
\qquad
h_S''(0)=\frac{\pi^2}{2\sqrt8\,\Gamma(\frac54)^2}.
\]

\emph{Step 3: the planar side as \(a\to0^+\).}  By
Lemmas~\ref{lem:positive-source} and~\ref{lem:four-kernels} with
\(\lambda=1\),
\[
\begin{split}
H&=C\,\Phi_{0,0}(1;y),\\
H'&=-\mathcal W_1(a;1)=-aC\,\Phi_{1,0}(1;y),\\
H''&=\mathcal W_2(a;1)=2C\,\Phi_{1,1}(1;y).
\end{split}
\]
As \(y\downarrow0\), monotone convergence and
\(\int_0^\infty s^{2x-1}(1+s^2)^{-x-z}\dd s=\frac12B(x,z)
=\frac{\Gamma(x)\Gamma(z)}{2\Gamma(x+z)}\)
\cite[equations~(5.12.1) and (5.12.3)]{NISTHandbook2010} give
\(\Phi_{0,0}(1;y)\to\frac12B(\frac14,\frac14)=\Gamma(\frac14)^2/(2\sqrt\pi)\)
and \(\Phi_{1,1}(1;y)\to\frac12B(\frac34,\frac34)=\Gamma(\frac34)^2/\sqrt\pi\).
The substitution \(s=u/y\) gives
\(a\Phi_{1,0}(1;y)=\sqrt2\int_0^\infty\e^{-u}u^{1/2}(y^2+u^2)^{-1/2}\dd u\);
the integrand is bounded by \(\e^{-u}u^{-1/2}\), so by dominated
convergence this tends to \(\sqrt2\,\Gamma(\frac12)=\sqrt{2\pi}\).  Using
\(\Gamma(\frac14)\Gamma(\frac34)=\pi\sqrt2\),
\(\Gamma(\frac54)=\frac14\Gamma(\frac14)\) and \(\Gamma(\frac12)=\sqrt\pi\)
\cite[equations~(5.5.3), (5.5.1), (5.4.6)]{NISTHandbook2010}, the limits
of \(H\), \(H'\), \(H''\) as \(a\to0^+\) are \(h_S(0)\),
\(h_S'(0)=-\pi\) and \(h_S''(0)\).

\emph{Step 4: uniqueness.}  The function \(W=H-h_S\) is smooth on
\((0,\infty)\), satisfies \(W'''=-a^2W'-aW\), and \(W,W',W''\to0\) as
\(a\to0^+\).  Fix \(a_1>0\).  For \(0<a\leq a_1\), the energy
\(E=W^2+W'^2+W''^2\) satisfies \(E'\leq(2+a_1^2+a_1)E\), by
\(2|uv|\leq u^2+v^2\).  Hence \(\e^{-(2+a_1^2+a_1)a}E(a)\) is
nonincreasing on \((0,a_1]\) and tends to \(0\) as \(a\to0^+\), so
\(E=0\) there.  Thus \(H=h_S=h\) on \((0,\infty)\).
\end{proof}

The identity of Lemma~\ref{lem:bessel} also follows from
Lemma~\ref{lem:positive-source} and the Dixon--Ferrar formula
\cite[entry~6.518]{GradshteynRyzhik2015}: substitute \(s=\sinh t\) in
\(\Phi_{0,0}(1;y)\), use
\(K_{1/2}(z)=\sqrt{\pi/(2z)}\,\e^{-z}\), take \(\nu=\frac14\) and
\(x=a^2/4\) in the formula, and use
\(Y_{1/4}=J_{1/4}-\sqrt2\,J_{-1/4}\).

\section{Identification with the quartic recurrence}
\label{sec:completion}

Define
\begin{equation}
V_n=\frac{\fh_{n+1}}{\fh_n},
\qquad
v_n=\frac{V_n}{a}
\qquad(n\geq0),
\label{eq:ratios}
\end{equation}
and \(V_j=v_j=0\) for \(j<0\).  By Corollary~\ref{cor:positive-norms},
\(v_n>0\) for every \(n\geq0\).  We derive
\eqref{eq:quartic-recurrence}, specializing
\cite[Lemma~3.4 and equations~(3.1)--(3.3)]{FelderHoppe2025}.

If \(g\) has \(C_4\)-weight \(\ell\), then
\begin{equation}
\sigma(zg)=-\tau(\ell)z\,\sigma g.
\label{eq:sigma-multiplication}
\end{equation}
For \(\ell=0,1,2\) this follows by comparing the twist factors in
weights \(\ell\) and \(\ell+1\); for \(\ell=3\) the weight returns to
\(0\), and \(\eta^4=-1\) supplies the sign \(\tau(3)=-1\).

Let \(L=\partial_z+\ii z^3\), where
\(\partial_z=\frac12(\partial_{\operatorname{Re}z}
-\ii\partial_{\operatorname{Im}z})\).  Since
\(\partial_z\overline{\sigma g(z)}=0\) and
\(\partial_z\rho_a(z)=(-a\bar z+\ii z^3)\rho_a(z)\), integration by
parts in \eqref{eq:pairing}, with no boundary terms because of the
Gaussian decay, and \eqref{eq:sigma-multiplication} give, for \(g\) of
weight \(\ell\),
\begin{equation}
(Lf,g)_a
=a\int_{\C}f\,\bar z\,\overline{\sigma g}\,\rho_a\dd A
=-\tau(\ell)a(f,zg)_a.
\label{eq:ibp-pairing}
\end{equation}

The polynomials \(zP_n\) and \(LP_n\) have weights \(n+1\) and \(n-1\)
modulo \(4\), degrees \(n+1\) and \(n+3\), and leading coefficients
\(1\) and \(\ii\).  If \(j\leq n-7\) and \(j\equiv n+1\pmod 4\), then
\(\deg LP_j<n\), so \eqref{eq:ibp-pairing} gives
\(0=(LP_j,P_n)_a=-\tau(n)a(P_j,zP_n)_a\).  If \(j\leq n-5\) and
\(j\equiv n-1\pmod 4\), then \(\deg zP_j<n\), so
\((LP_n,P_j)_a=-\tau(j)a(P_n,zP_j)_a=0\).  Hence
\begin{equation}
zP_n=P_{n+1}+c_nP_{n-3},
\qquad
LP_n=\ii P_{n+3}+d_nP_{n-1},
\label{eq:polynomial-recurrences}
\end{equation}
where terms with negative indices are omitted; equivalently,
\(c_0=c_1=c_2=0\), and \(c_n\) for \(n\geq3\) and \(d_n\) for
\(n\geq1\) are the coefficients displayed.  Among any four
consecutive integers exactly one is congruent to \(3\) modulo \(4\), so
\eqref{eq:rho-sign} gives
\[
\varrho(n)=\tau(n)\varrho(n-3)\quad(n\geq3),
\qquad
\varrho(n)=-\tau(n-1)\varrho(n-1)\quad(n\geq1).
\]
Pairing \eqref{eq:polynomial-recurrences} with \(P_{n-3}\) (for
\(n\geq3\)) and \(P_{n-1}\) (for \(n\geq1\)), and using
\eqref{eq:ibp-pairing} and Hermitian symmetry, we obtain
\[
\begin{split}
\ii\varrho(n)\fh_n
&=(LP_{n-3},P_n)_a
=-\tau(n)a(P_{n-3},zP_n)_a,\\
c_n
&=\frac{(zP_n,P_{n-3})_a}{\varrho(n-3)\fh_{n-3}}
=\frac{\ii}{a}\frac{\fh_n}{\fh_{n-3}},\\
d_n
&=\frac{(LP_n,P_{n-1})_a}{\varrho(n-1)\fh_{n-1}}
=-\tau(n-1)a
\frac{(P_n,zP_{n-1})_a}{\varrho(n-1)\fh_{n-1}}
=a\frac{\fh_n}{\fh_{n-1}};
\end{split}
\]
the first line holds because the leading term of \(LP_{n-3}\) is
\(\ii P_n\), and the last because that of \(zP_{n-1}\) is \(P_n\).
Thus \(c_m=\frac{\ii}{a}V_{m-3}V_{m-2}V_{m-1}\) for every \(m\geq0\),
including \(c_m=0\) for \(m<3\).

Applying the first relation in \eqref{eq:polynomial-recurrences} three
times shows that the coefficient of \(P_{n-1}\) in \(z^3P_n\) is
\[
c_n+c_{n+1}+c_{n+2}
=\frac{\ii}{a}V_{n-1}
\left(
V_{n-2}V_{n-3}
+V_nV_{n-2}
+V_{n+1}V_n
\right).
\]
Let \(n\geq1\).  The coefficient of \(P_{n-1}\) in \(\partial_zP_n\) is
\(n\), since \(\partial_zP_n=nz^{n-1}+\cdots\) lies in the weight space
of \(P_{n-1}\).  Comparing coefficients of \(P_{n-1}\) in
\(LP_n=\partial_zP_n+\ii z^3P_n\) and using \(d_n=aV_{n-1}\), we obtain,
with \(m=n-1\geq0\),
\begin{equation}
V_m\left[
1+\frac1{a^2}
\left(
V_{m-1}V_{m-2}
+V_{m+1}V_{m-1}
+V_{m+2}V_{m+1}
\right)
\right]
=\frac{m+1}{a}.
\label{eq:V-recurrence}
\end{equation}
This is the case \(d=4\) of \eqref{eq:FH-recurrence}, and with
\(V_m=av_m\) it is \eqref{eq:quartic-recurrence}.

Finally, \(P_0=1\), \(P_1=z\), and \(P_2=z^2\).  By
Lemma~\ref{lem:bessel}, \(h(a)=\int_\C\rho_a\dd A\), and
differentiation under this absolutely convergent integral gives
\[
h'(a)=-\int_{\C}|z|^2\rho_a(z)\dd A(z),
\qquad
h''(a)=\int_{\C}|z|^4\rho_a(z)\dd A(z).
\]
With \eqref{eq:twist-sign} and \(\varrho(0)=-\varrho(1)=\varrho(2)=1\),
this gives
\[
\fh_0=h,\qquad \fh_1=-h',\qquad \fh_2=h''.
\]
Hence \(h(a)>0\) and \(h'(a)<0\), so \eqref{eq:initial-values} is well
defined, and \eqref{eq:ratios} reproduces it.  Thus \((v_n)\) extends
the initial values and satisfies \eqref{eq:quartic-recurrence} for every
\(n\geq0\).  If \((\widetilde v_n)\) is another real sequence with these
properties and \(\widetilde v_j=v_j\) for \(j\leq m+1\), then
\eqref{eq:quartic-recurrence} at index \(m\) is linear in
\(\widetilde v_{m+2}\) with coefficient
\(\widetilde v_m\widetilde v_{m+1}=v_mv_{m+1}>0\), so
\(\widetilde v_{m+2}=v_{m+2}\).  Induction proves uniqueness.  This
proves Theorem~\ref{thm:main}: part~(i) is
Corollary~\ref{cor:positive-norms}, and part~(ii) has just been shown.

\section{Uniqueness of the positive solution}\label{sec:unique}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:unique}]
Fix \(\epsilon>0\) and let \((v_n)\) and \((v_n')\) be positive solutions
of \eqref{eq:quartic-recurrence}.  Put \(T_n=v_nv_{n+1}v_{n+2}\) and
\(T_{-1}=T_{-2}=0\), and similarly \(T_n'\).  Since
\(v_n(v_{n-1}v_{n-2}+v_{n+1}v_{n-1}+v_{n+2}v_{n+1})=T_{n-2}+T_{n-1}+T_n\),
the recurrence reads
\begin{equation}
v_n+T_n+T_{n-1}+T_{n-2}=(n+1)\epsilon,
\qquad n\geq0.
\label{eq:integrated}
\end{equation}

\emph{Step 1: a priori bounds.}  All terms in \eqref{eq:integrated} are
nonnegative, so \(v_n\leq(n+1)\epsilon\) and \(T_n\leq(n+1)\epsilon\).
Hence, bounding each factor of the bracket in
\eqref{eq:quartic-recurrence} by these estimates (for \(n=0,1\) the
products containing a negative index vanish), the bracket is at most
\(1+\epsilon^2[n(n-1)+n(n+2)+(n+2)(n+3)]=1+3\epsilon^2((n+1)^2+1)\leq
K(n+1)^2\) with \(K=1+6\epsilon^2\), and therefore
\begin{equation}
v_n\geq\frac{c}{n+1},
\qquad
c=\frac{\epsilon}{K}.
\label{eq:lower-bound}
\end{equation}
The same bounds hold for \(v'\).  With
\(\delta_n=\log(v_n'/v_n)\), it follows that
\(|\delta_n|\leq\log K+2\log(n+1)\).

\emph{Step 2: exact linearization.}  For \(x,y>0\), the logarithmic mean
is \(L(x,y)=(y-x)/(\log y-\log x)\) if \(x\neq y\) and \(L(x,x)=x\);
equivalently \(L(x,y)=\int_0^1x^{1-t}y^t\dd t\).  Let
\(a_n=L(v_n,v_n')\) and \(b_n=L(T_n,T_n')\), so that
\(v_n'-v_n=a_n\delta_n\) and \(T_n'-T_n=b_nD_n\) with
\(D_n=\delta_n+\delta_{n+1}+\delta_{n+2}\).  A logarithmic mean lies
between its arguments, so by Step~1
\begin{equation}
a_n\geq\frac{c}{n+1},
\qquad
0<b_n\leq(n+1)\epsilon.
\label{eq:log-means}
\end{equation}
Subtracting \eqref{eq:integrated} for the two solutions gives
\begin{equation}
a_n\delta_n+b_nD_n+b_{n-1}D_{n-1}+b_{n-2}D_{n-2}=0,
\qquad n\geq0,
\label{eq:linearized}
\end{equation}
where terms with negative indices are omitted.

\emph{Step 3: a localized energy identity.}  Let \((h_n)_{n\geq0}\) be real
with finite support.  Multiplying \eqref{eq:linearized} by
\(h_n^2\delta_n\), summing over \(n\), and collecting the terms with the
same \(b_m\) gives
\[
\sum_{n\geq0}a_nh_n^2\delta_n^2
+\sum_{m\geq0}b_m
\Bigl(\sum_{r=0}^2\delta_{m+r}\Bigr)
\Bigl(\sum_{r=0}^2h_{m+r}^2\delta_{m+r}\Bigr)=0.
\]
For real \(z_0,z_1,z_2\) and \(h_0,h_1,h_2\),
\[
\Bigl(\sum_{r}z_r\Bigr)\Bigl(\sum_{r}h_r^2z_r\Bigr)
=\Bigl(\sum_{r}h_rz_r\Bigr)^2
+\sum_{0\leq r<s\leq2}(h_r-h_s)^2z_rz_s .
\]
Dropping the nonnegative squares, using \(2|z_rz_s|\leq z_r^2+z_s^2\) and
\eqref{eq:log-means}, we obtain
\begin{equation}
c\sum_{n\geq0}\frac{h_n^2\delta_n^2}{n+1}
\leq\frac\epsilon2\sum_{m\geq0}(m+1)
\sum_{0\leq r<s\leq2}(h_{m+r}-h_{m+s})^2
\bigl(\delta_{m+r}^2+\delta_{m+s}^2\bigr).
\label{eq:localized}
\end{equation}

\emph{Step 4: a power weight.}  Let \(0<p<1\), put
\(w_n=(n+1)^{-p}\), and
\[
S_p=\sum_{n\geq0}\frac{\delta_n^2}{(n+1)^{1+2p}},
\]
which is finite by the logarithmic bound of Step~1.  For an integer
\(R\geq1\), the sequence \(h_n=(w_n-w_R)_+\) is supported on \(n<R\), and
\(|h_i-h_j|\leq|w_i-w_j|\).  By the mean value theorem,
\(|w_{m+r}-w_{m+s}|\leq p(s-r)(m+1)^{-p-1}\).  In \eqref{eq:localized}
the three pairs \((r,s)\) contribute the squared distances \(1\), \(4\),
\(1\), so the right-hand side is at most
\[
\frac{\epsilon p^2}{2}\sum_{m\geq0}
\frac{5\delta_m^2+2\delta_{m+1}^2+5\delta_{m+2}^2}{(m+1)^{1+2p}}
\leq\frac{\epsilon p^2}{2}\cdot12\cdot27\,S_p
=162\,\epsilon p^2S_p,
\]
where we used \((m+r+1)/(m+1)\leq3\) and \(1+2p\leq3\).  As
\(R\to\infty\), \(h_n\uparrow w_n\), and monotone convergence on the
left of \eqref{eq:localized} gives \(cS_p\leq162\,\epsilon p^2S_p\).
Choosing \(p=1/(18\sqrt K)\) makes \(162\,\epsilon p^2=c/2\), so
\(cS_p\leq\frac c2S_p\).  Since \(S_p<\infty\), \(S_p=0\), hence
\(\delta_n=0\) and \(v_n'=v_n\) for every \(n\).
\end{proof}

With \(a_n,b_n\) held fixed, \eqref{eq:linearized} is the
Euler--Lagrange equation of the quadratic form
\(\sum_na_n\delta_n^2+\sum_nb_n(\delta_n+\delta_{n+1}+\delta_{n+2})^2\).
In the variables \(u_n=\log v_n\), \eqref{eq:integrated} itself is the
coordinatewise stationarity condition of the formal energy
\(\sum_n\bigl[\e^{u_n}+\e^{u_n+u_{n+1}+u_{n+2}}-(n+1)\epsilon\,u_n\bigr]\),
whose increments along finitely supported variations are well defined
and strictly convex.  Convexity alone does not compare two infinite
sequences; the power-weighted estimate of Steps~3 and~4 controls the
boundary at infinity.
